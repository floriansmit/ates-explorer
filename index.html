Other / custom profile works anywhere without Dutch or Danish location services. Define named aquifers and aquitards with top/base depths, a reference elevation and named datum, optional Kh and evidence. Layers cannot overlap. Update the preview, select aquifers and apply them to the system. User inputs are not regional-model or field-verified data. Custom profile definitions are retained in scenario exports.

Netherlands uses TNO REGIS II and PDOK address search. Denmark uses GEUS’ FOHM service, KDS Adressevælger for house-number location and an OpenStreetMap background. Danish coordinates are ETRS89 / UTM 32N (EPSG:25832). Each FOHM response is separated into named model groups: FOHM, DK-model or deeper geological models. Groups are never merged into one overlapping column. The selected group and retrieval time are preserved in exports; the point service does not publish a model version.

Danish intervals contain geometry but no hydraulic conductivity. Candidate sands and chalk/limestone can be applied to the system with Kh left empty; the simulation remains blocked until all selected intervals have positive Kh. Values entered here are user-supplied, not GEUS model values. Record a pumping-test reference or identify an assumption in each interval’s evidence field. Chalk is represented only as an effective porous medium: fracture flow is not resolved.

The Danish point response does not declare its vertical datum, so elevations remain labelled as source elevations, not Dutch NAP or an assumed DVR90. Confirm the source model’s datum for design. Model coverage varies; an empty response never triggers substitute geology. FOHM’s separate thermal-conductivity estimates are not automatically applied because their method and units have not been verified in this app.

REGIS II retrieval

A live OPeNDAP point query retrieves top/base elevations (m NAP, positive up), Kh/Kv (m/day), transmissivity (m²/day), resistance (days) and available conductivity standard deviations. Grid spacing is 100 m. Missing values remain unknown. The app uses the cell containing the entered RD coordinates, and exports version, cell bounds, query URL and retrieval time. Layer codes are retained exactly as supplied by TNO.

Select one or more aquifer units, then apply them to the system. Each screen defaults to the full unit but its top/base and Kh can be edited independently in the system box. Only the sand screens count towards net thickness. This assumes the screens are fully saturated. Check groundwater head, feasible screen intervals, chemistry and local boreholes before interpreting flow capacity. No permit or suitability verdict is made.

Fast monthly screening

Q = 2π K b s / ln(R/r), converted from m³/day to m³/hour. This isolated confined-well estimate ignores skin, well losses and interference. Flow through a pair is not doubled. Source power = 1.163 × Q × ΔT. Building heat extraction = delivered heat × (1 − 1/COP). Cooling is direct/free cooling. Monthly energy is limited by capacity × calendar hours; this is an optimistic ceiling, not hourly dispatch. Backup is excluded.

The fast-screening warm/cold inventories and annual loss setting are diagnostic only and are not used in the transient solver.

Transient thermal model

The experimental solver advances bulk aquifer heat storage on a uniform Cartesian grid using conservative first-order upwind advection and central conduction. Exact integrated radial doublet face flows are superimposed on uniform regional Darcy flow. Face dispersion is approximated as water heat capacity × absolute face flow × dispersivity / cell size. Bulk saturated volumetric heat capacity is entered directly and already includes water and rock. Local thermal equilibrium is assumed.

Injection and extraction share equal water flow. Heating extracts at the warm well and injects at the cold target; cooling reverses flow and injects at the warm target. The solver limits timestep using a positivity bound. Monthly loads are spread uniformly across days; heating receives priority, then cooling, then idle transport. This does not reproduce hourly peaks or simultaneous heat recovery.

Production temperature determines available ΔT. Heating COP varies using a Carnot fraction calibrated to the entered nominal COP at ambient groundwater and heating supply temperatures; it is bounded to 1.1–9. This is not a manufacturer performance curve. Direct cooling stops when production temperature plus heat exchanger approach reaches the building cooling supply temperature. Plant-side return temperatures and operating modes are idealised; injection targets are imposed.

Each selected aquifer has a separate homogeneous 2D thermal field and ambient-temperature open boundaries. Transmissivity is summed across screens: T = Σ(Kh × screened thickness). Total well-pair flow is split in proportion to interval transmissivity, assuming a shared head change. Production temperatures mix by water flow into a common header; injection targets and operating hours are shared. Clay and other gaps contribute neither screened thickness nor transmissivity. Inter-layer heat or groundwater leakage, differing initial heads and off-cycle crossflow are not represented. Each repeated well pair remains independent. Wells are snapped to grid cell centres; actual simulated spacing is reported. No vertical heat leakage, density/buoyancy, clogging, geochemistry or inter-pair interactions are modelled. A 20 m cell can smear near-well temperatures: compare finer grids and larger domains. Plan views and cross-sections show actual saved states from day zero to the final year, with daily, weekly or month-end output. Output cadence is separate from adaptive solver timesteps. The cross-section extrudes each aquifer’s calculated centreline temperature through its screened thickness, using a horizontally extended local geological column. It is a visualisation of depth-averaged fields, not a vertically resolved heat model. Display-only block averaging limits snapshot memory; it does not change solver resolution.

The export reports numerical energy-balance residual (storage change − net boundary energy − well energy) per pair. Numerical conservation and invariance tests check implementation; they do not establish predictive validity. Benchmark against analytical cases, MODFLOW 6 GWE and field observations before design use.

Validation: MODFLOW or OPM?

The app combines analytical hydraulic screening with a custom numerical thermal solver. Numerical conservation is necessary but insufficient to establish predictive accuracy. Our recommended next benchmark is MODFLOW 6 GWF/GWE for low-temperature groundwater ATES, using matched boundaries, geometry and injection schedules. Compare production temperatures, plume extent, recovered energy and pumping heads, then repeat with finer grids, larger domains, aquitard heat transfer and groundwater flow. Site pumping and thermal observations are needed for calibration.

OPM Flow also supports thermal simulation and temperature-dependent properties; it is an alternative, particularly where reservoir-style well controls and deeper geothermal workflows are important. Neither simulator automatically confirms a simplified model: discrepancies must be understood. No MODFLOW or OPM comparison has yet been run for this app.
